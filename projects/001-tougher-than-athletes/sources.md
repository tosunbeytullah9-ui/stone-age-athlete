# Sources — 001 Why Ancient Humans Were Tougher Than Today's Athletes

- Macintosh, Pinhasi & Stock (2017), *Science Advances* — Neolithic women's arm bones 11–16% stronger than
  Cambridge University women's rowing crew, ~30% stronger than typical students; grinding grain up to 5 h/day;
  rowers training twice a day, ~120 km/week.
  https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/prehistoric-womens-manual-work-was-tougher-than-rowing-in-todays-elite-boat-crews
- Ryan & Shaw (2014), *PNAS* — ~20% higher trabecular bone density in foragers vs later farmers.
  https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/12/141222165033.htm
- Raichlen et al. (2017), *American Journal of Human Biology* — Hadza ~135 min/day MVPA, 14× US adults.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27723159/
- Morin & Winterhalder (2024), *Nature Human Behaviour* — ~400 accounts of persistence hunting.
  https://phys.org/news/2024-05-theory-humans-evolved-ability-distances.html
- Pontzer et al. (2012), *PLOS ONE* — Hadza daily energy expenditure similar to Westerners.
  https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/07/120725200304.htm
- Stieglitz et al. (2019), *eLife* — fracture prevalence among Tsimane (numbers to verify before use).
  https://elifesciences.org/articles/48607
