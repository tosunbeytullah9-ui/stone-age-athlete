# Sources — How Your Can Opener Cuts Steel With One Hand
<!-- From the preparation package (prep.yaml). Claims are in claims.yaml. -->

**Question:** How does an ordinary can opener cut steel with one hand, and why did it arrive decades after the can?

**What the evidence says:** A modern rotary opener combines a lever (the handles clamp the wheels onto the rim), a wedge-edged cutting wheel that concentrates force into very high pressure on thin tinplate, and a crank (a lever turning round) that drives a serrated feed wheel under the rim so the can turns itself through the cutter. Early cans were heavy wrought iron and were opened with tools at hand; dedicated openers appeared from 1855 (Yeates) and 1858 (Warner), Lyman's wheel came in 1870 but needed a centre pivot, the 1925 Star feed wheel made the rim the track, and Bunker's 1931 design is the layout most hand openers still use.

**This video's angle:** Most can-opener content is either trivia ('cans came first') or usage tips; this video explains the physics of all three simple machines inside it with exact animated diagrams, and ties the history to the physics: the tool could only work once the can got thin.
