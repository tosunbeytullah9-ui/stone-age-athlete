# Sources — Case 001: You Don't Need 8 Glasses of Water a Day
Every claim is in claims.yaml (panel: İddialar) and linked to library/sources.yaml. All claims start as verified: false.
Valtin 2002 (Am J Physiol) — no scientific proof every person must drink 8 x 8; 1945 FNB origin. https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/ajpregu.00365.2002 · press: https://geiselmed.dartmouth.edu/news/2002_h2/08aug2002_water.shtml
APS, The Physiologist 2021 — 1945 quote "Most of this quantity is contained in prepared foods"; Stare at least six glasses; thirst is the primary driver. https://www.physiology.org/publications/news/the-physiologist-magazine/2021/july/the-science-of-hydration
CDC NCHS Data Brief 242 (2016) — IOM AI 3.7 L / 2.7 L total water; plain water 30% (men) / 34% (women). https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db242.htm
Yamada et al. 2022, Science — 5,604 people, 26 countries; turnover ~1-6 L/day; 3.2 / 2.7 L worked example; athletes ~1 L more; metabolic water ~10%; 8 x 8 "not backed by objective evidence". https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9764345/
Killer et al. 2014, PLOS ONE — 50 habitual male coffee drinkers; coffee hydrates like water in moderation. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0084154
Almond et al. 2005, NEJM — 2002 Boston Marathon, 488 runners, 13% hyponatremia; weight gain / overdrinking. https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa043901 · https://www.amssm.org/marathon-runners-are-at-r-p-43.html
ACSM 2007 position stand — weigh before/after; prevent >2% body-weight loss. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17277604/
Lawrence Journal-World 2002 — bread ~35% water (confirm with USDA FoodData Central). https://www2.ljworld.com/news/2002/aug/26/prof_says_water/
